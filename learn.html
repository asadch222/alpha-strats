Reading a chart

Candle

One bar on the chart covering a set time. Green means price ended higher than it started; red means lower.

Timeframe

How much time each candle covers, such as 1 hour or 1 day. Short ones show detail, long ones show the big picture.

Trend

The general direction of price. Up when highs and lows keep rising, down when they keep falling.

Support (floor)

A price level where buyers have stepped in before, so falls often pause there.

Resistance (ceiling)

A price level where sellers have appeared before, so rises often stall there.

Breakout

When price pushes clearly through a ceiling or floor. It often starts a bigger move.

Volume

How much was traded. Moves on high volume are taken more seriously than moves on low volume.

Volatility

How much price jumps around. High volatility means bigger swings in both directions.

Indicators

Moving average (EMA)

The average price over recent candles, drawn as a line. It smooths out noise so the trend is easier to see.

RSI

A 0 to 100 score of how hard price has been pushed. Under 30 is "oversold", over 70 is "overbought".

Bollinger Bands

A band around the average price. Price near the top edge is stretched up; near the bottom, stretched down.

MACD

Compares short and long momentum. Above zero, buyers are gaining strength; below zero, sellers are.

Supertrend

A line that sits under price in an uptrend and above it in a downtrend. A flip suggests the trend changed.

Setup score

Our own 0 to 100 rating of how closely a market matches a classic buying pattern. Higher is cleaner.

Market mood

Fear and greed

A 0 to 100 mood score. Low means traders are scared and selling; high means they are excited and buying.

Funding rate

A small fee swapped between traders every few hours. Positive means most are betting on a rise.

Open interest

The total value of bets still open. Rising open interest means more money is flowing into that market.

Long and short

Going long is betting the price will rise. Going short is betting it will fall.

Long/short ratio

How many traders are long compared with short. Very lopsided crowds are often wrong at turning points.

Liquidity

How easily you can buy or sell without moving the price. Busy markets have more of it.

Strategy results

Backtest

Running a strategy's rules over past prices to see how it would have done. It is not real trading.

Win rate

The share of trades that made money. A low win rate can still be profitable if the wins are much bigger than the losses.

Profit factor

Total money won divided by total money lost. Above 1 is profitable; above 2 is strong.

Drawdown

The biggest fall from a peak in the account before it recovered. It shows how painful the bad stretch was.

Stop-loss

A pre-set price where a losing trade is closed automatically to limit the damage.

Leverage

Trading with borrowed money. 5x means gains and losses are five times bigger.

Risk per trade

How much of the account is put at risk on one trade. Small numbers keep one bad trade from hurting much.

Slippage and fees

The small costs of real trading: the exchange's charge and getting a slightly worse price than planned.

What you are looking at

Perpetual contract

A way to trade the price of something without owning it, with no end date.

Tokenised stock

A digital contract that follows a company's share price and trades around the clock.

USDT

A digital dollar. One USDT is designed to stay worth one US dollar.

Mark price

A fair price worked out from several exchanges, used so one odd trade cannot distort things.

Index price

The average price of the real asset across major exchanges.

Market value (market cap)

Price multiplied by the number of coins in circulation: what the whole coin is worth.
